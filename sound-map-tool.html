What is this tool doing?

Any sound you hear — a voice, a clap, a musical note — is actually made of many simple sine waves layered on top of each other. This tool separates that sound into its component frequencies and shows you how strong each one is. Use your microphone for live capture, or upload a recorded audio file. When you are ready, freeze the data and download it to analyze the math behind what you heard.

Math Extension — questions to explore with your data

  • Graph frequency (x) vs. amplitude (y). What does the shape of that graph tell you about your sound?
  • Which frequency has the highest amplitude? That is the dominant wave in your sound. What sine function could model it?
  • Calculate the ratio of each frequency's amplitude to the maximum amplitude. What does it mean when ratios are close together vs. far apart?
  • If you capture two different sounds — a voice and a clap — how do the frequency distributions compare? What statistics would you use to describe the difference?
  • Fourier's theorem says any periodic signal is a sum of sine waves. Write a general expression: f(t) = A₁sin(2πf₁t) + A₂sin(2πf₂t) + … and substitute in values from your data.

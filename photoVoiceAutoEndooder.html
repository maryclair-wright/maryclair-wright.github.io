What the model is doing

Autoencoder

A small neural network made of adjustable internal connections. During training, it's shown every row in the file, over and over, and each time it tries to rebuild the row from a version of itself squeezed down to a single number. It compares its rebuild to the real row, then adjusts its own connections slightly to do better next time. Nobody tells it what a normal row looks like. It just keeps practicing on whatever rows it's given, so it naturally gets best at rebuilding whichever pattern shows up the most, since that's what it practices on most often.

Reconstruction error

Every row gets a reconstruction error: the difference between what the row actually was and what the model rebuilt from its compressed version. The network produces this same way, for every row, without exception. It isn't deciding a row is normal or unusual here, it's only measuring how close its rebuild came.

How a row gets flagged

Calling a row an outlier happens after training, as a separate step. Every row's error gets compared to every other row's error in the same file, and whichever rows sit far above the rest get flagged. That comparison is a rule applied on top of the network's output. The network itself has no concept of normal or outlier, only how well or poorly it could rebuild each row.

What flagging doesn't mean

A flagged row is a statement that the row is statistically rare in this file, nothing more. The model doesn't know why it's rare, whether it matters, or what it means. It can't tell a real problem from a harmless one-off from something it just hadn't seen enough times yet. Deciding what a flagged row actually means is a human job the model can't do.

The math

Reconstruction error is really just a distance: how far off the model's rebuilt row landed from the real row, added up across every feature in that row. Landing too high or too low both count as being off by the same amount, being off by a little barely registers, and being off by a lot stands out a lot more than twice as much.

To decide what counts as far, the tool looks at every row's distance in the file and finds the typical range those distances fall into. A row gets flagged when its distance is well beyond that typical range, further off than almost every other row in the file.
